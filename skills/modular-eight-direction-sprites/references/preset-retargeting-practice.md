# Preset motion, Mixamo and skeletal transfer

Iteration 8, 2026-10-11. User's “Maximo” is interpreted as Adobe **Mixamo**. The user also permits presets from other providers. [Source audit](retargeting-source-audit.json) records exact reading scope; [executable experiment](../../../research/sprite-animation/preset-retarget-v8/README.md) records what was actually tested. This is a study draft, not an approved animation seed.

## What the sources establish

**Adobe Mixamo documentation.** A useful humanoid auto-rig/animation service: custom unrigged FBX/OBJ/ZIP, existing rigs via FBX, marker placement and skeleton mapping. Adobe cautions about extreme proportions, big appendages/clothing/props, disconnected parts and non-neutral poses. Account sign-in is required. Its Blender add-on exposes an IK control rig and baking. No Mixamo account operation, clip download or add-on execution was performed here. Do not invent one universal bone count, download FPS or root convention for all files.

**Pinocchio, Baran and Popović, 2007.** Skeleton embedding and skin attachment are different problems. The selected paper sections describe heat-based weights, ambiguous knee/elbow placement, difficult proportions and material-blind deformation. This supports explicit joint placement and deformation review, particularly for rigid armor versus cloth. The paper's example success rate is not a reliability estimate for our character or today's Mixamo service.

**Skeleton-Aware Networks, Aberman et al., 2020.** Hierarchy-aware operators reduce compatible skeleton graphs to a shared representation. Selected ablations/discussion report end-effector errors and remaining IK cleanup; differing reference poses and object interaction remain difficult. Learning a motion map does not certify a weapon grasp. This network was not trained or run.

**Contact-Aware Retargeting of Skinned Motion, Villegas et al., 2021.** Selected method sections add mesh self-contact, penetration, foot-contact and skeleton-motion objectives; source contacts are estimated. The limitations describe conflicting objectives, possible loss of motion character, stiff correction and fixed joint-count restrictions. A hand that reaches a point can still intersect the torso. This method was not executed.

**Footskate Cleanup, Kovar, Schreiner and Gleicher, 2002.** The method works from footplant annotations, then computes ankle configurations, root/leg adjustments and surrounding-frame blends. It explicitly permits leg-length changes in some cases, and incorrect contact annotations can introduce artifacts. Our proposed sprite rig instead keeps anatomical limb lengths fixed and reports unreachable targets; this is a different bounded policy, not a reproduction of their solver.

**Kinematic Motion Retargeting for Contact-Rich Anthropomorphic Manipulations, Lakshmipathy et al., 2025.** The selected input/method/limitation sections use actual hand/object geometry, contact areas, complete trajectories and artist-marked correspondences. They also report wobble, finger contortion and failures around changed objects/pregrasp/release. A wrist socket is weaker evidence than finger/palm surface contact. We did not reconstruct contacts or run this method.

**Two patents.** US8797328B2, originally assigned to Mixamo, describes proxy geometry, salient points, reference-skeleton fitting, weights, characterization and motion transfer. US20200334892A1 describes representative mesh segmentation, key points, bone generation and weight transfer. Selected descriptions and claims were read, not implemented; no current-service implementation or rights clearance is inferred. They help separate stages of an auto-rig system; they do not supply a tested “make every sprite correct” recipe.

**Pinned Rokoko Blender implementation.** The inspected retarget operator retains target head/tail/roll, builds target-oriented helper bones in a source copy, constrains rotations/location and bakes. Root-based scaling and removal of object-level animation also appear. A README MIT badge conflicts with the inspected LGPL-3.0 license text. No code was copied, installed or executed. Preserve real object/root motion before choosing an import/cleanup strategy.

**Godot 4.4 documentation.** Matching names is insufficient: rest orientation and pose conventions matter. Its rest fixer, mapping, root/hips track exceptions and motion-scale normalization are engine-specific. Overwriting axes or filtering accessory tracks can damage intended motion. This was documentation study, not a decision to change our game engine.

**KayKit's current author page.** Lists 161 humanoid animations, Medium/Large rig families, locomotion/melee/ranged/general/tool sets, free CC0 FBX/glTF and paid Blender sources. The author warns that transfer to another character may not look good. This is an additional candidate library: no archive, bones or individual clips were inspected this iteration. Quaternius's audited author file supplied the actual eight-preset experiment.

## Proposed production route for our sprites

1. **Preserve the source.** Record provider/version/license, immutable hash, actual hierarchy, units, object transform, neutral pose, clip and input timestamps. Separate source-rate study from deliberately sparse production keys. Do not infer animation frames from the Blender scene FPS after import.
2. **Calibrate anatomical frames.** Map pelvis, multiple spine joints, clavicles, arms, legs, feet and available fingers explicitly. Check neutral pose, joint axes/roll and bend planes. Bone labels and screen left/right are different concepts. A general transfer needs calibrated rest-basis conversion; directly copying local quaternion components is valid only for demonstrated matching conventions. Reject missing, duplicate, cross-side or cyclic mappings.
3. **Choose a motion family.** Walking/running require stride speed and supported-foot review; starts/stops/jump/land are distinct phases. A travelling sword lunge differs from a compact planted cut. Root travel must become deliberate world displacement or a documented in-place conversion with compensating trajectory/contact work. Do not delete it to make a sheet fit.
4. **Block the target body.** Transfer intent and phase order to original proportions. Adjust root/pelvis, distributed torso rotation, shoulders, knees and free arm together. If an arm cannot reach the weapon target, move the target or re-author body/shoulder action within joint limits. Do not stretch the limb or flip an elbow to claim success. Solving each limb independently is only a diagnostic.
5. **Solve geometry contacts.** Author actual heel/sole/toe support patches and active intervals. Lock only the declared support during those intervals; preserve release, heel roll and stepping. For weapons define palm orientation, hilt axis/radius, thumb opposition, finger wrap, blade plane and clearance against clothing/body. One-handed ownership stays anatomical right. Two-handed families also require the second grip and shoulder reach. Different hilt sizes need compatibility or different grip poses.
6. **Bake and reopen.** Verify final evaluated bone motion, weights, root trajectory and surface contacts in the saved source, including transitions. Review every requested direction with fixed camera/registration. A pass on ankle/wrist coordinates does not certify sole/finger geometry, weight transfer or native appeal.
7. **Produce replaceable parts.** Bind complete body/head/hair/costume pieces to the same accepted target rig. Preserve hidden content, mask rules and depth ordering. Add armor/cloth-specific deformation and restrained secondary motion after primary mechanics pass. Do not rely on Mixamo's whole-character upload to establish interchangeable item coverage.
8. **Choose sparse exposures, then draw.** Select actual anticipation/load/strike/recovery keys and walk contact/down/passing/up keys per motion. Preserve explicit holds, limb ownership and trajectory. Use our original style for redraw and native-scale review; derive trail timing from the accepted sword trajectory on the same clock. Existing rejected boards remain rejected.

## What the new experiment proves, and where it stops

Eight actual CC0 presets yield **339** evaluated source samples. The new guide scales parent-local offsets with different leg, torso, shoulder, arm and finger factors while preserving source rotations and root/pelvis movement. Its rest axes/topology match the source by construction: it does **not** solve cross-rig rest calibration or skinning. The target is a fresh generic proportion skeleton, not our character's finished proportions or an original animated mesh.

Identity FK reconstructs measured source joint heads within **3.63e-6 source units**; target non-pelvis parent-child offsets vary by at most **1.33e-6**. Pelvis-to-root is intentionally excluded from the rigid-limb check because the source animates that logical offset, notably during roll/jump. No animation channel is removed to make the test pass.

The synthetic trajectory targets deliberately differ from the target's shorter arms/wider hips: wrist goals use source chest-relative displacement ×0.80; ankle goals use source root-relative displacement ×0.64. They are **not** measured hilt or floor contacts. Independent two-segment diagnostics preserve segment lengths and source bend half-planes, report unreachable goals and retain residuals. For the right arm, walk has **41/41** unreachable goals and sword attack **47/47**; maximum sword residual is **0.03694 source units**. Jog/sprint right-arm goals are reachable in all samples, but that does not approve their feet, style or grip.

The rejection is useful: changing proportions and copying rotations cannot preserve an independently specified endpoint trajectory automatically. The next target implementation must negotiate body, prop and support constraints together, bake them to an actual original rig, and pass visible palm/foot/native reviews. No contact cleanup, original motion repair or production release is claimed here.
