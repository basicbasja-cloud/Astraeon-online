Astraeon V3 — Golden Swordsman identity, layered art, and motion master prompt

Create one consistent original male fantasy Swordsman across actions, directions, frames, and interchangeable equipment. Use the two owner-supplied high-detail character images as the current visual-quality and costume-shape reference, and the approved V2 crop only as the existing identity seed. Use V3 structural renders for pose, camera, timing, registration, and motion. Use open Ragnarok source projects as practical references for character drawing, frame conventions, equipment and hair separation, directional actions, palette changes, and readable combat effects. Reuse any source art only when its own license allows it; the server or client code license does not automatically cover official game data. Keep the Astraeon master artwork original and build it through the full-frame-first, same-frame-cutout workflow.

IDENTITY AND COSTUME
- Youthful storybook warrior with tousled silver/lavender-white hair and a broad swept fringe partly hiding the eyes.
- Bronze and polished warm-gold armor over a split ivory tunic, dark navy/olive sleeves and trousers, teal waist cloth, brown leather belts, gloves, and tall dark boots.
- A straight, narrow, one-handed steel sword with a sculpted warm bronze/gold hilt, gripped in the anatomical right hand.
- Include the reference's mustard-gold scarf and long golden cloak as a distinct `BackAccessory` cosmetic. The default beauty render wears it. A no-cloak configuration must remove it cleanly without changing the body, weapon, pose, camera, framing, or any other layer.
- Preserve the elevated fantasy-sprite camera, body proportions, stable root, and eight real unmirrored directions in this order: S, SW, W, NW, N, NE, E, SE.

ART QUALITY
- Match the owner reference's clear, high-detail illustrated fantasy RPG finish: crisp silhouette, controlled ink edges, individually readable hair locks, clean face and eye planes, carved armor seams, layered leather, cloth folds, metal reflections, and a sharp readable sword edge.
- Draw the art at the largest clean source resolution available, with enough deliberate detail to survive reduction to the 160px runtime cell. Keep the character large in that cell and preserve a consistent height, foot baseline, root, contour weight, and palette in every frame and view.
- Use deliberate hand-painted highlights and shadows with clean separations between materials. Silver-lavender, mustard-gold, bronze, ivory, navy/olive, teal, and dark brown remain identity colors.
- Do not use blur, airbrush haze, soft-focus edges, speckle/noise, washed-out low-contrast paint, fuzzy antialiasing, photorealism, a 3D-render appearance, plastic shine, thick black cartoon outlines, modern tactical elements, or a background.

AUTHORING ORDER: FULL FRAME FIRST, CUTOUT SECOND
- Paint only from a motion and guide that have passed visual review. Preserve their exact action, frame order, direction, camera, root, and timing; do not redesign the motion while painting. If the guide misses the requested action, reject it and replace the motion before making a full master.
- First paint one complete character master frame for each of 16 motion-guide frames and eight views. The master shows the whole finished character in that exact pose, including its head and hair, body and clothes, weapon, and cape. Keep the full 8×16 master sheet coherent in design, scale, lighting, and frame registration.
- From that exact master sheet, cut each frame into labelled parts: `BaseBody`, `HeadHair`, `Outfit`, `Weapon`, `BackAccessory`, `Headgear`, and `WeaponLight`. The cutouts must come from the same pixels and frame; do not draw a separate full action sheet for each part.
- Keep each cutout in its original frame coordinates. Preserve its alpha edge and exact camera/root placement. Layer cutouts back together and compare against the master before making replacement assets.
- Use the cutout silhouettes as templates for future hair, clothing, weapon, and accessory designs. Paint concealed base surfaces where a replacement would otherwise reveal a hole; record those reconstructed pixels separately from the exact visible cutout.
- Make blade light or a short trail a separate transparent `WeaponLight` cutout. If the master does not include it, add it later in the same per-frame coordinates without repainting or moving the character or sword.

REAL, SWAPPABLE ART LAYERS
- Cut out `BaseBody`, `HeadHair`, `Outfit`, `Weapon`, `Headgear`, `BackAccessory`, and `WeaponLight` into separate transparent assets. `HeadHair` is the initial head-and-hair cutout group; later hair choices use that exact outline and frame placement as their template.
- The master is an authoring reference, not a runtime substitute. Keep every runtime part independently selectable. The first cutout set may contain only the visible pixels from the master; paint missing concealed surfaces before enabling any equipment or hair swap that would expose them.
- Keep overlaps and draw order intentional. Weapon changes must leave the hand and pose unchanged; the cape must be independently toggleable; the assembled parts must reproduce the exact master before replacement art is added.
- Create one aligned cutout sheet per layer and action from the matching master sheet. Preserve transparent pixels, source frame order, and the shared root. Do not recenter, mirror, or independently transform a cutout.
- Follow the RO client pattern: keep `WeaponLight` independent from `Weapon`, and synchronize its action, facing, frame, offset, and draw order with the body and sword. For the SE BasicAttack, use the owner's rough 4×4 sample as a loose shape and thickness cue with an original gradient from muted plum-gray shadows through lavender silver to a pale warm-ivory core. Follow the approved source motion's actual sword-tip path and strike timing; do not force the effect or character into a horizontal or sideward cut. Keep each effect frame registered to the matching blade pose, keep the sword readable, and do not bake the effect into `Weapon`, `BaseBody`, or `Outfit`.
- Every frame must use the actual source motion and order. Do not invent, duplicate, mirror, or independently recenter frames. Maintain one palette and consistent identity across the complete set.

ANIMATION QUALITY
- Make every action read as a human carrying and controlling a sword: credible balance, foot contact, torso mechanics, visible weight transfer, consistent grip, and smooth arcs.
- Idle: alert sword-ready stance with restrained breathing and a small natural weight shift.
- Walk: alternating planted footfalls, steady torso, relaxed sword arm, and no exaggerated march or foot sliding.
- BasicAttack: preserve the approved motion source as captured, whether it is an overhead, diagonal, or lateral strike. Keep readable anticipation, wind-up, body weight transfer, contact, follow-through, and recovery to guard. Do not reshape it into a side cut. For the selected Rokoko sword action, follow the raised-sword wind-up and diagonal downward strike, and keep the sword grip and blade on the guide's hand path. Let the trail's broad section arrive with the actual blade contact and taper through follow-through, using the owner's rough 4×4 sample only as a loose shape and thickness cue. Keep attack strength in the body, not a loose wrist-only wave. Avoid deep forward bows, flailing, stiffness, and toy-like posing.
- Keep the character stable through the whole action: register one shared root to a continuously planted support foot, do not recenter or rescale frames independently, and keep painted head/body/limb proportions steady. When the source art's first pose differs from the motion guide, fit the source pose to the guide before applying subsequent motion deltas. For 2D IK, choose the bend solution nearest the previous frame and allow only smooth perspective-aware segment changes within 0.78–1.12× the master length; do not lock one projected elbow/knee side when the 3D limb turns through camera depth. Inspect the head, shoulders, waist, hands, knees, feet, and sword together in the animated review.
- Treat character continuity and pose fidelity as separate pass gates. A fixed output root or normalized silhouette height does not pass continuity if independently painted frames change the character's body proportions, costume shapes, cape silhouette, or scale. Build from one consistent high-resolution master with registered frame-specific cutouts or a shared articulated surface; redraw from the exact matching Blender pose only when the result also passes the adjacent-frame identity and silhouette review.
- Audit the painted sword axis, grip point, and blade length against every corresponding Blender weapon proxy before painting `WeaponLight`. Record the angular difference per frame, inspect the wind-up-to-contact frames at native cell size, and reject any sequence where the blade snaps or the trail follows a different pose source than the painted sword.

SPRITE AND RUNTIME CONTRACT
- Structural guides use 320x320 pixels, root `(160,264)`, eight unmirrored views, and shared `Idle`, `Walk`, and `BasicAttack` frame timing. Use 16–32 real poses for typical actions and allow up to 64 poses for short, fast actions when native source cadence still pops; sample true motion subframes in Blender rather than duplicating or blending painted images. Looped actions cover `[0,1)` without a duplicated endpoint and the final pose must flow into frame 0. Production BasicAttack is a one-shot that recovers to guard and then returns to Idle. A looping attack GIF is allowed only as an explicit owner-review artifact to inspect its seam; label it as a review loop, never as runtime playback, and reject it if the last-to-first pose pops. Runtime cells remain 160x160 with root `(80,132)`. Target a 112px visible character height within each cell, keeping the root and foot baseline fixed to improve fine-detail clarity without changing world-space character size.
- Sheets use directions as columns and chronological frames as rows. Every layer uses the same per-cell registration and transparent canvas; all 128–512 poses form a coherent action.
- Keep the full sword, hair, cloak, and all effects inside their frame bounds. Separate blade light into a dedicated `WeaponLight` part and include empty/transparent frames outside the strike.
- Validate composition after loading interchangeable hair, outfit, weapon, headgear, cloak, and no-cloak variants in the Astraeon renderer. Check occlusion, anchors, palette consistency, frame timing, silhouettes, foot contacts, weapon continuity, and attack readability.
